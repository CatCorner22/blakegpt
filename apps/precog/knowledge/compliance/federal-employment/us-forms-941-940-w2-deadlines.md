# Employment tax returns and wage statements

**Rule.** The practice must calendar filing and employee-statement deadlines separately from tax deposits. The practice must not assume a filing extension also extends payment time.

**Exact text.**
> "on or before the last day of the first calendar month following the period for which it is made" (26 CFR 31.6071(a)-1(a)(1)).

**Thresholds and deadlines.**
- Form 941 is ordinarily due by the last day of the month after the quarter; Form 940 is ordinarily due January 31 after the year (26 CFR 31.6071(a)-1(a)(1), (c); IRS Publication 15, tax calendar guidance).
- Furnish employee Forms W-2 by January 31 after the year; file the Social Security Administration copies by January 31 under the ordinary rule (26 CFR 31.6051-1(d)(1); 31.6071(a)-1(a)(3)(i)).
- Fully timely deposits can permit filing by the 10th day of the next month beyond the ordinary filing month for the covered employment-tax returns (26 CFR 31.6071(a)-1(a)(1), (c)).

**Exceptions.**
- Weekend and holiday adjustments and special final-return or early-statement rules require separate review. Filing extensions are restricted, not automatic; the wage-statement extension provisions point to separate governing rules (26 CFR 31.6051-1(d); 31.6071(a)-1(a)(3); 31.6081(a)-1).

**Office checklist.**
- At year-end and each quarter-end, confirm filing obligations, reconcile wages and deposits, and save filing receipts; owner dentist signs off. IRS form pages and Publication 15 are guidance, not law.

**Records to keep.** Keep returns, wage statements, reconciliation work, and payment records for at least 4 years under IRS guidance (IRS Publication 15, Recordkeeping).
