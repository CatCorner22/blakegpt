# Worker classification under FLSA and federal tax rules

**Rule.** The practice must assess actual working relationships under the applicable legal test before treating a worker as a contractor. The practice must not rely on a contract label alone or treat federal tax classification as deciding FLSA status.

**Exact text.**
> "whether the worker is either economically dependent on the potential employer for work or in business for themself" (29 CFR 795.105(a)).

**Thresholds and deadlines.**
- The fetched eCFR retains the 2024 rule's economic-realities analysis, including opportunity for profit or loss, investments, permanence, control, integral work, and skill and initiative (29 CFR 795.105; 795.110(b)).
- DOL enforcement guidance dated May 1, 2025 states that WHD no longer applies the 2024 rule's analysis in investigations and instead uses Fact Sheet 13 and the identified opinion-letter guidance; this enforcement position does not itself repeal the regulation (DOL Field Assistance Bulletin 2025-1, Enforcement Guidance).
- The Federal Register document published February 27, 2026 proposes restoring a modified version of the 2021 approach; it is proposed, not final (91 FR 9932, summary).
- IRS guidance separately examines behavioral control, financial control, and the parties' relationship (IRS Independent Contractor or Employee guidance, Common law rules).

**Exceptions.**
- No single contractual label settles the economic-realities analysis. DOL enforcement policy, operative regulatory text, a proposed rule, and IRS tax guidance have different legal roles; do not collapse them into a single test (29 CFR 795.105; 795.110; cited guidance and proposal).

**Office checklist.**
- Before engagement and whenever duties or control change, document scheduling, tools, investment, outside business, and pay facts; owner dentist and advisers sign off separately on wage-hour and tax treatment.

**Records to keep.** No retention rule in this source.
