# Tennessee: only a licensed dentist may own an active dental practice

**Rule.** An owner of an active Tennessee dental practice must be a dentist licensed in Tennessee. A dentist must not practice as an employee of an entity not engaged primarily in the practice of dentistry, except the employer, government, nonprofit, hospital and charitable-clinic exceptions. A lay management company may provide services to the practice but must not own it.

**Exact text.**

> "(1) For any licensed dentist to practice dentistry as an employee of any person or other entity not engaged primarily in the practice of dentistry; or (2) For an owner of an active dental practice to be other than a dentist duly licensed to practice in this state." (T.C.A. 63-5-121(a)).

> "the estate or agent of such dentist may employ another dentist or dentists for a period of not more than two (2) years to provide services to patients until the practice can be sold or otherwise disposed of or closed." (T.C.A. 63-5-121(b)).

**Thresholds and deadlines.**

- Death or substantial disability of the owner dentist: estate or agent may employ dentists for not more than 2 years (T.C.A. 63-5-121(b)).

**Exceptions.**

- Dental services an employer regularly makes available to its employees; services by a state agency or subdivision, a nonprofit organization, or a hospital (T.C.A. 63-5-121(a)).
- Charitable clinics meeting all 6 standards in T.C.A. 63-5-121(c)(2), with a written contract that does not restrict independent professional judgment (T.C.A. 63-5-121(c)(1)).

**Office checklist.**

- Confirm the practice entity's owners of record are all Tennessee-licensed dentists; recheck at any ownership change.
- Have counsel review any MSO agreement for control over clinical decisions, ownership options, or fees that function as profit sharing.
- Owner dentist signs off once a year.

**Records to keep.** No retention rule in this source.
