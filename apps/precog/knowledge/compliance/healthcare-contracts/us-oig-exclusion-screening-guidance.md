# Exclusion screening: OIG monthly-screening guidance and the State Medicaid monthly database check

**Rule.** The practice may choose its LEIE screening frequency because no statute or regulation requires a provider to check the LEIE. The practice may follow OIG guidance (guidance, not law) by screening at hire and every month. The State Medicaid agency, not the provider, must check the LEIE and EPLS at least monthly under 42 CFR 455.436(c)(2).

**Exact text.**

> "Providers are not required by statute or regulation to check the LEIE." (OIG Special Advisory Bulletin, May 8, 2013, Frequency of Screening).

> "OIG updates the LEIE monthly, so screening employees and contractors each month best minimizes potential overpayment and CMP liability." (same).

> "Check the LEIE and EPLS no less frequently than monthly." (42 CFR 455.436(c)(2), duty of the State Medicaid agency).

**Thresholds and deadlines.**

- OIG guidance: screen before hiring or contracting and periodically; monthly is OIG's stated best practice (SAB 2013).
- State Medicaid agency: LEIE and EPLS at least monthly (42 CFR 455.436(c)(2)).
- EPLS is now part of SAM.gov; the regulation still says EPLS (inference; SAM.gov not fetched).

**Exceptions.**

- None stated in the source.

**Office checklist.**

- Monthly: search every employee and contractor name on the LEIE (oig.hhs.gov) and on SAM.gov exclusions; print or save the result.
- At hire: run both checks before the start date.
- Office manager runs; owner dentist initials the monthly log.

**Records to keep.** No retention rule in this source. Keep monthly screening results with the CMP records (see us-cmp-excluded-person-contracting).

**Related.** us-cmp-excluded-person-contracting, us-exclusion-effect-no-payment

**Not verified.** Whether TennCare or its dental benefits manager requires providers by contract to screen LEIE and SAM monthly was not verified: no TennCare provider agreement, dental benefits manager provider manual, or Tennessee rule requiring provider-side monthly screening was fetched. The OIG bulletin is guidance, not law.
