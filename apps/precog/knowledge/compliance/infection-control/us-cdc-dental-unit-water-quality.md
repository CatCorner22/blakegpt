# Dental unit waterlines: 500 CFU/mL treatment and monitoring (CDC guidance)

**Rule.** This is guidance; no Tennessee rule or federal regulation found in this section sets a waterline limit for dental offices. The office may treat waterlines to 500 CFU/mL or less, test as the unit or product manufacturer directs, and use sterile water or saline for surgery.

**Exact text.**

> "All dental units should use systems that treat water to meet drinking water standards (i.e., ≤500 CFU/mL of heterotrophic water bacteria). Independent reservoirs—or water-bottle systems—alone are not sufficient." (CDC Dental Unit Water Quality page).

> "Dental unit water quality must also be monitored, or tested, routinely as recommended by the equipment manufacturer." (CDC Best Practices for Dental Unit Water Quality, Recommendations).

> "Use sterile saline or sterile water as a coolant/irrigant when performing surgical procedures." (CDC Dental Unit Water Quality page, Key Recommendations).

> "Because the recommended value of <500 CFU/mL cannot be achieved by using this method, other strategies should be employed." (2003 Guideline, Strategies To Improve Dental Unit Water Quality).

**Thresholds and deadlines.**

- Limit: 500 CFU/mL or less heterotrophic bacteria.
- Testing interval: per the manufacturer; CDC sets no number.
- Flushing alone does not meet the limit (2003 Guideline).

**Exceptions.**

- Surgical procedures need sterile water or saline through a sterile delivery device, not unit water.

**Office checklist.**

- Follow the treatment product schedule (shock and maintenance tablets or in-line cartridge).
- Test each unit on the manufacturer schedule with a lab or in-office kit; log result per chair.
- Over 500 CFU/mL: shock, retest, and do not use the unit until it passes (per manufacturer).

**Records to keep.** No retention rule in this source. Keep test results with the sterilization logs for at least 2 years by office policy.
