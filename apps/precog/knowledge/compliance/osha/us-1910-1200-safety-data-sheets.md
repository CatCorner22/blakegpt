# Safety data sheets and employee access

**Rule.** The employer must have an SDS in the workplace for each hazardous chemical used and ensure immediate, barrier-free employee access during each shift. The employer must obtain a missing SDS from the supplier as soon as possible.

**Exact text.**
> "Employers shall have a safety data sheet in the workplace for each hazardous chemical which they use." (29 CFR 1910.1200(g)(1)).

**Thresholds and deadlines.**
- Maintain access during each work shift; electronic systems must create no barrier to immediate access (29 CFR 1910.1200(g)(8)).
- Obtain a missing SDS as soon as possible (29 CFR 1910.1200(g)(6)(iii)).
- Federal update effective July 19, 2024; employer substance-related updates as necessary by November 20, 2026 and mixture-related updates as necessary by May 19, 2028 (29 CFR 1910.1200(j)(1), (2)(ii), (3)(ii)).

**Exceptions.**
- Traveling employees may use SDSs at the primary workplace if they can immediately obtain information in an emergency. Electronic access and grouped information are allowed if required information remains accessible (29 CFR 1910.1200(g)(8)-(10)).
- Federal transition permits the current text, the text revised as of July 1, 2023, or both until the applicable compliance dates (29 CFR 1910.1200(j)(4)). General coverage exclusions also appear in (b); do not assume all dental products are excluded.

**Office checklist.**
- Practice workflow: Amanda: match incoming chemicals to SDSs and test access for each shift, including when the usual computer is unavailable. Owner dentist: approve the access arrangement.

**Records to keep.** Keep SDSs accessible while chemicals are used (29 CFR 1910.1200(g)(1), (8)); exposure-record preservation is addressed separately in us-1910-1020-exposure-medical-records-access.
