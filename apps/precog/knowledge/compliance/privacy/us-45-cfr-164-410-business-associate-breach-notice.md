# Business associate breach notice

**Rule.** A business associate must notify the covered practice after discovery of a breach of unsecured PHI without unreasonable delay and no later than 60 calendar days. The practice may require a shorter contractual report period; that choice is not the federal outside limit.

**Exact text.**
> "without unreasonable delay and in no case later than 60 calendar days after discovery of a breach." (45 CFR 164.410(b)).

> "If the statement is in writing and specifies the time for which a delay is required, delay such notification, notice, or posting for the time period specified by the official" (45 CFR 164.412(a)).

> "If the statement is made orally, document the statement, including the identity of the official making the statement, and delay the notification, notice, or posting temporarily and no longer than 30 days from the date of the oral statement, unless a written statement as described in paragraph (a) of this section is submitted during that time." (45 CFR 164.412(b)).

**Thresholds and deadlines.**
- Discovery includes knowledge obtainable through reasonable diligence and knowledge of employees, officers, or agents other than the perpetrator (164.410(a)(2)).
- Provide affected individuals' identities to the extent possible and other available patient-notice details with the report or promptly afterward as available (c).

**Exceptions.** 
- A law-enforcement official must state that notice, notification, or posting would impede a criminal investigation or damage national security before the delay rule applies (164.412).
- For a written statement specifying the required delay, delay for the time specified by the official (164.412(a)).
- For an oral statement, document it, including the official\'s identity; delay temporarily for no longer than 30 days from the oral statement unless a qualifying written statement arrives during that time (164.412(b)).
- A contract does not turn 60 calendar days into a waiting period.

**Office checklist.**
- For each law-enforcement delay, Amanda preserves the statement, official\'s identity, oral-statement date if applicable, and delay end date; the privacy official approves the grounds and tracks any timely written statement (164.412).
- On each BA incident report, Amanda records the BA discovery date and promptly alerts the privacy official.
- The privacy official signs off on follow-up data and the practice's own notice decision; Amanda checks contractual reporting duties at onboarding and renewal.

**Records to keep.** 164.410 gives no fixed BA retention period; 164.414(b) assigns the BA a burden to prove notification or a no-breach basis. The covered practice keeps its required notification documentation for 6 years from creation or last effective date, whichever is later (164.530(j)(1)(iv), (2)); the BA must also check its contract and applicable security-documentation duties.
