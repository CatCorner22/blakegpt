# Noncompetes for Tennessee dentists and other licensed providers

**Rule.** An employing or contracting practice may use the statutory reasonableness rule for a covered provider’s post-service restriction if both parties sign a written restriction that meets the statutory duration and either the geographic or facility test. An employer must check the separate annualized-compensation floor before requesting or enforcing a provider employee’s noncompete.

**Exact text.**

> “two (2) years or less” (T.C.A. § 63-1-148(a)(2) — unofficial Justia mirror)

> “A ten-mile radius from the primary practice site” (T.C.A. § 63-1-148(a)(2)(A)(i) — unofficial Justia mirror)

> “chapters 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9 and 11 of this title” (T.C.A. § 63-1-148(c) — unofficial Justia mirror)

> “does not impose an upper limit on reasonability of geographic restrictions” (No. 3:24-cv-00678, Doc. 61, at 26 (M.D. Tenn. June 24, 2026))

**Thresholds and deadlines.**

- Dentists and licensed dental hygienists are within Title 63, chapter 5. Confirm the role’s license before applying the rule (T.C.A. §§ 63-1-148(c), 63-5-108(b)–(c)).
- For statutory deemed reasonableness, the signed post-service restriction runs for at most 2 years and may cover up to the greater of 10 miles from the provider's primary practice site or the county of primary practice. Alternatively, a restriction with no geographic limit may address only facilities where the entity provided services while the provider worked for it (T.C.A. § 63-1-148(a)(1)–(2)).
- For the separate post-July 1, 2026 employee compensation rule, the floor is $70,000 in annualized compensation. The Public Chapter applies to proceedings occurring and agreements entered into, renewed, or amended on or after July 1, 2026 (2026 Tenn. Pub. Acts ch. 934, §§ 1, 3).
- A federal trial court treated the statutory geographic limits as a deemed-reasonable standard and found a wider restriction reasonable on the facts of a podiatry case (No. 3:24-cv-00678, Doc. 61, at 25–26 (M.D. Tenn. June 24, 2026)).

**Exceptions.**

- The statute excludes physicians who specialize in emergency medicine (T.C.A. § 63-1-148(d)).
- On the purchase or sale of a provider practice or all or substantially all of its assets, the restriction must remain reasonable in duration and area. The agreed duration and area receive a rebuttable presumption of reasonableness (T.C.A. § 63-1-148(b)).

**Office checklist.**

- At drafting, renewal, or enforcement, have the owner and counsel confirm the provider’s license, employment classification and compensation, primary site, facility list, and signatures. Route practice sales for separate review.

**Records to keep.** No retention rule in this source.
