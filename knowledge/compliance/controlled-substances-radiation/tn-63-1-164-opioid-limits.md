# Tennessee opioid supply and dose limits

**Rule.** A dentist must not treat a patient with more than a 3-day supply of an opioid or more than 180 total morphine milligram equivalents, unless an exception in § 63-1-164 applies. When the statutory conditions are met, the dentist may treat with up to a 10-day supply and 500 total MME.

**Exact text.**

> "a healthcare practitioner shall not treat a patient with more than a three-day supply of an opioid and shall not treat a patient with an opioid dosage that exceeds a total of one hundred eighty (180) morphine milligram equivalent dose." (T.C.A. § 63-1-164(b), Justia unofficial mirror).

> "the healthcare practitioner may treat the patient with no more than a ten-day supply and with a dosage that does not exceed a total of a five hundred (500) morphine milligram equivalent dose" (T.C.A. § 63-1-164, Justia unofficial mirror).

> "However, a healthcare practitioner shall not treat a patient with more than a fourteen-day supply of such an opioid" (T.C.A. § 63-1-164, Justia unofficial mirror).

> "A patient shall not be treated with an opioid more frequently than every ten (10) days" (T.C.A. § 63-1-164, Justia unofficial mirror).

**Thresholds and deadlines.**

- Default: 3-day supply, 180 total MME.
- Conditional: 10-day supply, 500 total MME.
- No more often than every 10 days (adverse-reaction exception).
- Cough and upper-respiratory opioid products: not more than a 14-day supply.

**Exceptions.**

- Exemptions listed in the source include active or recent cancer treatment, palliative care, hospice, sickle cell disease, severe burns and major physical trauma; check the statute text for each.

**Office checklist.**

- Dentist documents the reason, ICD-10 code and informed consent when exceeding 3 days; Amanda audits quarterly.

No retention rule in this source.
