# Sterilization monitoring: mechanical, chemical and weekly spore tests (CDC guidance)

**Rule.** This is guidance. The office may follow CDC: check mechanical readings every load, put a chemical indicator in every package, run a spore test with a matching control at least weekly and with every implant load, and keep records as state law requires (Tennessee: 2 years).

**Exact text.**

> "Monitor sterilizers at least weekly by using a biological indicator with a matching control (i.e., biological indicator and control from same lot number)" (2003 Guideline, Recommendations IX.F.6).

> "Use a biological indicator for every sterilizer load that contains an implantable device." (2003 Guideline, Recommendations IX.F.7).

> "Mechanical monitoring should be conducted for every sterilizer load." (CDC Best Practices for Sterilization Monitoring, Mechanical monitoring).

> "A chemical indicator should be used inside every package to verify that the sterilizing agent has penetrated the package and reached the instruments inside." (CDC Best Practices for Sterilization Monitoring, Chemical monitoring).

> "If the repeat spore test result is positive, remove the sterilizer from service and do not use until it has been inspected or repaired and re-challenged with spore tests in three consecutive fully loaded chamber sterilization cycles." (CDC Best Practices for Sterilization Monitoring, Sterilization failures).

> "Before placing the sterilizer back in service, rechallenge the sterilizer with biological indicator tests in three consecutive empty chamber sterilization cycles" (2003 Guideline, Recommendations IX.F.9.c).

**Thresholds and deadlines.**

- Spore test: at least weekly; every implant load.
- Mechanical: every load. Chemical: inside every package; outside too if the inside indicator is not visible.
- After a 2nd positive: 3 consecutive spore-test cycles before return to service. The 2024 page says "fully loaded"; the 2003 guideline says "empty chamber" (conflict noted).
- Recall: items back to the last negative spore test (2003 IX.F.9.b; 2024 page "when possible").

**Exceptions.**

- CDC sets no separate interval for part-time sterilizers ("CDC does not provide a separate recommendation for sterilizers that are used on a part-time basis").

**Office checklist.**

- Weekly spore test, same day each week; matching-lot control.
- Do not release packs if a gauge reading or indicator failed.
- After any positive: follow tn-0460-01-11-spore-testing-and-logs (that is the binding rule).

**Records to keep.** CDC: "Maintain sterilization records (i.e., mechanical, chemical, and biological) in compliance with state and local regulations" (2003 IX.F.10). Tennessee: at least 2 years (0460-01-.11(4)).
