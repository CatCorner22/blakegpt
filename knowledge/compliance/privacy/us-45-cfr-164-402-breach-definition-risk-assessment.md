# Breach definition and risk assessment

**Rule.** A covered practice or business associate must presume an impermissible PHI acquisition, access, use, or disclosure is a breach unless an exclusion applies or a documented assessment shows a low probability of compromise. The entity must support any decision not to notify with evidence.

**Exact text.**
> "Whether the protected health information was actually acquired or viewed" (45 CFR 164.402, Breach definition (2)(iii)).

**Thresholds and deadlines.**
- Assess at least 4 factors: nature/extent and identifiers, unauthorized recipient, actual acquisition/viewing, and mitigation (164.402, Breach definition (2)(i)–(iv)).
- Unsecured PHI is PHI not rendered unusable, unreadable, or indecipherable through the Secretary's specified technology/methodology (164.402).

**Exceptions.** 
- Exclusions cover qualifying good-faith within-authority workforce access, inadvertent sharing between authorized persons, and good-faith belief that the recipient could not reasonably retain the information; each exclusion has conditions (Breach definition (1)(i)–(iii)).
- A low-probability finding is not a general no-harm-to-patient test.

**Office checklist.**
- For every suspected incident, Amanda preserves facts and promptly alerts the privacy/security officials.
- The privacy official approves the documented exclusion or risk assessment and notification decision; Amanda tracks notice deadlines.

**Records to keep.** Keep notification evidence or the documented no-breach basis for 6 years from creation or last effective date, whichever is later (45 CFR 164.414(b); 164.530(j)(1)(iv), (2)).
