# Jury duty leave and pay

**Rule.** An employer must excuse a properly notified employee for qualifying jury service and must not retaliate for that service. A covered employer must pay usual compensation for qualifying jury service, subject to the statute’s limits.

**Exact text.**

> “exceeds three (3) hours” (T.C.A. § 22-4-106(a)(1) — unofficial Justia mirror)

> “usual compensation received from such employment” (T.C.A. § 22-4-106(b) — unofficial Justia mirror)

**Thresholds and deadlines.**

- The employee presents the summons to the immediate supervisor on the next day the employee works after receiving it. Excuse each day of federal or Tennessee jury service exceeding 3 hours (T.C.A. § 22-4-106(a)(1)).
- For a qualifying night or pre-court shift, excuse the shift just before the first day of service. After that, when jury duty exceeds 3 hours, excuse the next scheduled work period within 24 hours of the service day (subsection (a)(2)).
- A regular employer of 5 or more pays usual compensation for actual service and travel, with the option to deduct juror compensation (subsection (b)).

**Exceptions.**

- The pay duty excludes employers regularly employing fewer than 5 people and jurors temporarily employed for less than 6 months (T.C.A. § 22-4-106(b)). The statutory excuse and anti-retaliation provisions have no comparable size exception.

**Office checklist.**

- At each summons, log service and any preceding shift. Request an hours statement before the service if payroll needs one (T.C.A. § 22-4-106(c)).
- Have the payroll lead verify service, travel, and any juror-fee deduction.
