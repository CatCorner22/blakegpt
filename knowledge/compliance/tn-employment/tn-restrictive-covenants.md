# Tennessee employee noncompetes

**Rule.** An employer must not require, request, or enforce a noncompete against an employee earning less than $70,000 in annualized compensation. An employer seeking to enforce a noncompete must show a restraint reasonably limited to protecting its legitimate business interests.

**Exact text.**

> “the time and territorial limits involved must be no greater than is necessary to protect the business interests of the employer” (409 S.W.2d 361, 363 (Tenn. 1966) — court opinion, Justia mirror)

> “less than seventy thousand dollars ($70,000)” (2026 Tenn. Pub. Acts ch. 934, § 1, enacting T.C.A. § 50-1-211(a))

> “The Noncompete Rule is not in effect and it is not enforceable.” (Federal Trade Commission, Noncompete Rule, rule status (agency guidance))

**Thresholds and deadlines.**

- The amendment applies to proceedings occurring and agreements entered into, renewed, or amended on or after July 1, 2026 (2026 Tenn. Pub. Acts ch. 934, § 3).
- The $70,000 annualized compensation floor includes wages, salary, commissions, nondiscretionary bonuses, and other remuneration. Calculate annualized compensation for an hourly employee as the hourly rate multiplied by 40 and then by 52. An agreement executed in violation is void (2026 Tenn. Pub. Acts ch. 934, § 1, enacting T.C.A. § 50-1-211(a)–(c)).
- A post-termination time restraint of up to 2 years for former employees or independent contractors unrelated to a material sale or ownership interest is rebuttably presumed reasonable in duration. A longer restraint is presumed unreasonable. Evaluate territory, business need, consideration, employee hardship, and public interest separately (2026 Tenn. Pub. Acts ch. 934, § 1, enacting T.C.A. § 50-1-210(a), (b)(1); 409 S.W.2d at 363 (Tenn. 1966)).
- FTC agency guidance reports that the federal noncompete rule remains unenforceable after a federal district court order.

**Exceptions.**

- The statute specifies distinct timing presumptions for certain distributor and similar arrangements and for sale or ownership transactions. It permits courts to modify a covered restraint and preserves enforcement of confidentiality and customer or employee nonsolicitation agreements under the timing section (2026 Tenn. Pub. Acts ch. 934, § 1, enacting T.C.A. § 50-1-210(b)(2)–(3), (c)–(d)).
- Apply the licensed-provider statute when the agreement restricts covered professional practice.

**Office checklist.**

- Before requesting, renewing, amending, or enforcing a restriction, have the owner and counsel review annualized pay, the affected dates, provider licensure, the interest being protected, and the narrowest necessary scope. Recheck at departure.
