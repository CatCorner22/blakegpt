# Pumping breaks and private space

**Rule.** The practice must provide reasonable pumping breaks when needed and a private space other than a bathroom. The practice must pay for break time when the employee is not completely relieved from duty.

**Exact text.**
> "a place, other than a bathroom, that is shielded from view and free from intrusion from coworkers and the public" (29 U.S.C. 218d(a)(2)).

**Thresholds and deadlines.**
- Provide breaks for 1 year after the child's birth each time needed (29 U.S.C. 218d(a)(1)).
- Fewer than 50 employees does not create an automatic exemption; the employer must establish the specified undue hardship (29 U.S.C. 218d(c)).
- Before a lawsuit for failure to provide the space, the employee ordinarily must notify the employer and allow 10 days to comply; statutory exceptions apply (29 U.S.C. 218d(f)).

**Exceptions.**
- The pre-suit notice requirement has exceptions for specified discharge or an employer's stated refusal to comply. Statutory transportation exceptions exist; they do not describe an ordinary dental practice (29 U.S.C. 218d(d)-(f)).

**Office checklist.**
- For each request, arrange privacy and coverage without patient interruptions; check whether work continues during breaks; owner dentist signs off. DOL Fact Sheet guidance is not law.
