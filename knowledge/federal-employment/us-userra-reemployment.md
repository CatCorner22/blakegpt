# Military service protection and reemployment screening

**Rule.** The practice must not deny employment benefits because of protected uniformed service or retaliate for protected USERRA activity. The practice must assess reemployment rights when a service member returns, rather than treating military absence as an ordinary resignation.

**Exact text.**
> "any person, institution, organization, or other entity that pays salary or wages for work performed or that has control over employment opportunities" (38 U.S.C. 4303(4)(A)).

**Thresholds and deadlines.**
- Reemployment ordinarily requires advance notice, cumulative service within the statutory 5-year limit subject to exclusions, and timely reporting or application (38 U.S.C. 4312(a)-(c)).
- For service shorter than 31 days, the ordinary return point is the first full regularly scheduled work period on the first full calendar day after safe travel home and 8 hours of rest (38 U.S.C. 4312(e)(1)(A)).
- For service longer than 30 days but shorter than 181 days, ordinarily apply within 14 days after completion; for service longer than 180 days, ordinarily apply within 90 days (38 U.S.C. 4312(e)(1)(C)-(D)).

**Exceptions.**
- Military necessity or impossibility/unreasonableness can excuse advance notice. Specified service does not count toward the cumulative limit. Injury, illness, and circumstances beyond the person's control can alter return deadlines; missing a deadline does not automatically forfeit rights (38 U.S.C. 4312(b), (c), (e)). Employer defenses are limited and employer-burdened (38 U.S.C. 4312(d)).

**Office checklist.**
- When military absence is reported, log notice and expected service without demanding unauthorized advance paperwork; at return, obtain counsel's eligibility review; owner dentist signs off. DOL USERRA material is guidance, not law.
