# Spec checks

Verdicts on claims made by a third-party specification, checked against primary text fetched on 2026-11-18. Justia is an unofficial mirror of the Tennessee Code.

| claim | verdict | what the source says | citation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Tennessee bars lay corporations from practicing medicine or employing physicians, with exceptions for hospitals, community mental health centers and Title 48, Ch. 101, Part 6 entities | PARTLY | T.C.A. 63-6-204 defines the practice of medicine and lists entities that may employ physicians (for example employers treating their own employees in (c), community mental health centers in (d), FQHCs and rural health clinics, and hospitals under (f)(1) subject to county-population and specialty limits); it has no express sentence prohibiting lay corporations, and the exceptions are broader than the claim lists. 63-6-204(a)(2) excludes the practice of dentistry. For dentistry the operative rule is T.C.A. 63-5-121(a): only a Tennessee-licensed dentist may own an active dental practice | T.C.A. 63-6-204(a)(2), (c), (d), (f)(1); T.C.A. 63-5-121(a) (Justia unofficial mirror) |
| 2. T.C.A. 63-6-225 is a fee-splitting ban that bars percentage-of-collections management fees | FALSE | 63-6-225 applies to physicians; (a) bars dividing fees without the payer's knowledge and consent; (b) states it "does not prohibit a physician from compensating any independent contractor ... on the basis of a percentage of the physician's fees" if the percentage is reasonably related to the value of the goods or services; referral payments are prohibited; violation is a Class B misdemeanor | T.C.A. 63-6-225(a)-(c) (Justia unofficial mirror) |
| 3. Tennessee APRN rules require written protocols, supervisory visits every 30 days, and review of 20% of charts (or 100% within 10 days for controlled substances), citing T.C.A. 63-7-123, Rules 1000-04 and 0880-06 | PARTLY | Rule 0880-06-.02 (August 2004 revision) requires protocols ((5)), personal review of at least 20% of charts every 30 days ((8)), and a supervising-physician visit to any remote site at least once every 30 days ((9)); for controlled-drug prescriptions it requires a personal review every 10 business days of charts within 30 days ((7)(e)), not review of 100% of charts within 10 days. T.C.A. 63-7-123(b)(4) requires the nurse practitioner to keep the protocol at the practice site; 63-7-123(b)(7)(B) also allows up to 10 annual remote-site visits by HIPAA-compliant electronic means. No 20% or 30-day figure was found in Rule 1000-04 (2024-10-08 version) | Tenn. Comp. R. & Regs. 0880-06-.02(5), (7), (8), (9); T.C.A. 63-7-123(b)(4), (b)(7)(B) (Justia unofficial mirror) |
| 4. T.C.A. 63-6-214(b) and Rule 0880-02-.14 make the AMA Code of Medical Ethics a binding disciplinary standard | PARTLY | Rule 0880-02-.14(8) adopts the AMA Code of Medical Ethics "to the extent that it does not conflict with state law, rules or Board Position Statements", and "Violation of the Board's code of ethics shall be grounds for disciplinary action pursuant to T.C.A. § 63-6-214(b)(1)". T.C.A. 63-6-214(b) itself does not mention the AMA; (b)(1) lists "Unprofessional, dishonorable or unethical conduct". The rule binds physicians, not dentists | Tenn. Comp. R. & Regs. 0880-02-.14(8) (2025-11-13 version); T.C.A. 63-6-214(b)(1) (Justia unofficial mirror) |
| 5. T.C.A. 63-1-148 caps provider noncompetes at 2 years and the greater of a 10-mile radius or the county, with an exception for physicians practicing 6 or more years with the same employer | PARTLY | 63-1-148(a) deems a signed restriction reasonable if it lasts 2 years or less and covers the greater of a 10-mile radius or the county, or only the employer's facilities; (c) applies it to chapters 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9 and 11 (including dentists); (d) excludes emergency-medicine physicians. No 6-year exception appears. It is a safe-harbor test ("shall be deemed reasonable"), not an absolute cap | T.C.A. 63-1-148(a), (c), (d) (Justia unofficial mirror) |
| 6. T.C.A. 63-2-101 requires a mandatory 30-day transition/notice window on termination of a provider contract | FALSE | 63-2-101 governs release of medical records: a provider must give a patient a copy of records within 10 working days of a written request; it contains no contract-termination or 30-day transition rule | T.C.A. 63-2-101(a)(1) (Justia unofficial mirror) |
| 7. A business associate's breach notice to a covered entity has a 60-day federal outer limit | TRUE | "without unreasonable delay and in no case later than 60 calendar days after discovery of a breach" | 45 CFR 164.410(b) |
| 8. 45 CFR 164.508(b)(4) bars conditioning treatment on an authorization; compound authorizations are in (b)(3) | TRUE | (b)(4): "A covered entity may not condition the provision to an individual of treatment, payment, enrollment in the health plan, or eligibility for benefits on the provision of an authorization, except"; (b)(3): compound authorizations allowed only as listed | 45 CFR 164.508(b)(3), (b)(4) |
| 9. EKRA applies to all payers for recovery homes, clinical treatment facilities, and laboratories | TRUE | 18 U.S.C. 220(a) reaches "services covered by a health care benefit program" (defined by 18 U.S.C. 24(b), not limited to Federal programs) and referrals to "a recovery home, clinical treatment facility, or laboratory"; (d)(1) yields to the AKS for conduct the AKS prohibits | 18 U.S.C. 220(a), (d)(1), (e)(3) |
| 10. IRS accountable-plan rules are at 26 CFR 1.62-2 and require substantiation within a reasonable period, with safe-harbor day counts in (g) | TRUE | (g)(2)(i) fixed-date method: advance within 30 days, substantiation within 60 days, return within 120 days; (g)(2)(ii) periodic statement method: 120 days after a statement | 26 CFR 1.62-2(g)(2)(i), (ii) |
