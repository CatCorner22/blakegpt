# Tennessee medical waste: definition and special waste approval for landfill disposal

**Rule.** Discarded sharps used in patient care are "medical wastes" under Tennessee solid waste rules. A generator that sends medical waste to a permitted facility must have written special waste approval from the Department and must recertify every 3 years. The office may meet this through a licensed medical waste hauler that holds the approvals.

**Exact text.**

> "All discarded sharps (e.g., hypodermic needles, syringes, pasteur pipettes, broken glass, scalpel blades) used in patient care" (0400-11-01-.01(2), "Medical wastes" (e)).

> "an operator may not accept for processing or disposal at his facility any special waste unless and until specifically approved to do so in writing by the Department." (0400-11-01-.01(4)(b)).

> "Every three years, recertify the accuracy of the information on a form provided by the Department" (0400-11-01-.01(4)(c)4(i)).

> "Medical wastes that are not hazardous waste are subject to regulation under Tennessee’s solid waste regulations, Rule Chapter 0400-11-01." (TDEC Medical Waste web page).

**Thresholds and deadlines.**

- Recertify special waste approval every 3 years (0400-11-01-.01(4)(c)4(i)).
- Homeowner waste is excluded by TDEC policy (Policy Manual p. 129), not dental offices.

**Exceptions.**

- Medical waste that is also hazardous waste (e.g., some pharmaceuticals) falls under hazardous waste rules instead (TDEC Medical Waste page).

**Office checklist.**

- Keep a written contract with a medical waste hauler; confirm each year who holds the special waste approval.
- Keep hauler manifests or pickup receipts.
- Send waste pharmaceuticals and amalgam to the right stream, not red bags.

**Records to keep.** No generator retention period found in 0400-11-01-.01. Keep hauler manifests for at least 3 years by office policy (matches the 3-year recertification cycle).
