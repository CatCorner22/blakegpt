# Staff immunizations and TB screening (CDC/ACIP guidance)

**Rule.** The office may keep a written immunization policy listing hepatitis B, MMR, varicella and Tdap, screen every new hire for TB, and give annual TB education. These items are guidance; the only mandatory item here is the OSHA hepatitis B offer.

**Exact text.**

> "There is a written policy regarding immunizing DHCP, including a list of all required and recommended immunizations for DHCP (e.g., hepatitis B, MMR (measles, mumps, and rubella) varicella (chickenpox), Tdap (tetanus, diphtheria, pertussis)." (2016 Summary, DHCP Safety item 1).

> "All DHCP are screened for tuberculosis (TB) upon hire regardless of the risk classification of the setting." (2016 Summary, DHCP Safety item 2).

> "Postvaccination serologic testing is performed 1--2 months after administration of the last dose of the vaccine series using a method that allows detection of the protective concentration of anti-HBs (≥10 mIU/mL)." (ACIP 2011, Hepatitis B).

> "no routine serial testing in the absence of exposure or ongoing transmission, treatment for HCP diagnosed with LTBI, annual symptom screening for persons with untreated LTBI, and annual TB education of all HCP." (MMWR 2019;68(19), Summary).

> "If that test is negative, do another test 8–10 weeks after the last exposure." (MMWR 2019;68(19), Table).

**Thresholds and deadlines.**

- TB: baseline at hire; retest 8-10 weeks after last exposure if negative; annual education; annual symptom screen for untreated LTBI; no routine annual testing.
- Hep B titer: 1-2 months after last dose; immune at 10 mIU/mL or more.

**Exceptions.**

- MMR for staff born before 1957 is a "consider" item, not a firm recommendation, outside outbreaks (ACIP 2011).

**Office checklist.**

- At hire: collect vaccine records or titers; TB symptom check plus IGRA or TST; risk assessment.
- Each year: TB education in IPC training; offer flu vaccine.
- Keep these health records separate from the personnel file.

**Records to keep.** No retention rule in these CDC sources. If the record is part of the OSHA exposure medical record, keep it for employment plus 30 years (29 CFR 1910.1030(h)(1)(iv)).
