# OSHA and TOSHA knowledge cards

## Scope

Source-grounded reference cards for Amanda, office manager of a small Tennessee dental practice with 5–30 employees, covering workplace safety, employee training, reporting, and records. These are rule summaries and suggested office workflows, not a determination that the practice complies or individualized legal advice. Read Tennessee cards with federal cards: a current federal section is not proof of Tennessee adoption.

## How to use these cards

- Start with tn-tosha-state-plan-adoption, Tennessee recordkeeping exemptions, and severe-injury reporting. Routine log exemption does not excuse severe-event reports or Tennessee safer-sharps plan entries.
- Rule paragraphs summarize source duties. Office checklists are suggested delegation workflows, not additional legal deadlines or mandated signature requirements. Amanda maintains the workflow; the owner dentist approves applicability and controls.
- “Verified” means the stated rule, quoted text, and numerical requirements were checked against the retrieved source text. It does not mean the practice was inspected, the official Tennessee text was reachable, or the federal current text was compared word-for-word with Tennessee's adopted historical edition.
- “Unverified” is used conservatively for federal HazCom applicability in Tennessee and the unresolved dental-radiation compliance path. Supported federal text remains useful, but do not treat the flagged applicability question as resolved.
- All cards use last_verified: 2026-11-18 and review_days: 90. These identify the verification date and planned review interval, not statutory deadlines.
- The eCFR texts are current fetched texts, not historical reconstructions. Tennessee rules are Cornell LII mirrors because the official SOS copy returned CloudFront 403. Each affected card identifies the mirror and limitation.
- The tn.gov T.C.A. PDF is an agency copy and unofficial. The TOSHA memorandum is interpretation guidance, not a regulation. No names of individual agency staff are reproduced.

Related provisions are summarized within the requested cards rather than expanded into extra cards.

## Scope items not covered and why

- No requested card is omitted.
- TOSHA adoption of the federal HazCom update and later amendments is unresolved: the mirror adopts general-industry standards as of January 1, 2022, with exceptions, and the official SOS copy was blocked. The federal HazCom cards therefore remain unverified for Tennessee applicability.
- Tennessee dental X-ray registration rules and the actual practice's license/registration, survey, and agreement-state compliance evidence were not among the permitted sources. The radiation card states the federal monitoring triggers and conditional compliance provisions but does not decide the practice's actual compliance path.
- The source references the BLS request provision at 29 CFR 1904.42, but no separate text for that section was supplied. Only the references in 1904.1/.2 are summarized; no independent procedural details are invented.
- These cards do not comprehensively reproduce every subsection, technical table, incorporated standard, fire code, waste-disposal rule, or other dental safety topic. For example, the radiation card points to Table G-18 rather than converting its printed fractions into new decimal limits, and the extinguisher card identifies type-dependent testing rather than reproducing the whole table.

## Source notes

- The fetched sources support Tennessee's adoption date, the internal severe-reporting inconsistency, the TOSHA central number and second-report interpretation, safer-sharps plan documentation, annual HazCom refresher training and retention, dental partial exemption, posting duties, and the statutory-copy posting penalty.
- The nonmanufacturing chemical-list thresholds are not limited to chemicals over 55 gallons or 500 pounds. Tenn. Comp. R. & Regs. 0800-01-09-.11(2)(c)3 also supplies gas tests. The chemical-list card preserves that qualification; those liquid/solid cutoffs are not blanket HazCom exemptions.
- No permitted source established a rule to follow the 2024 federal HazCom text in Tennessee merely because it might be stricter. That suggestion is not adopted. The fetched federal text also contains later amendments and transition dates; it is not just the original 2024 publication.
- The Tennessee reporting summary in -.02(2)(a)1 still says hospitalization of 3 or more employees, while -.05(1) expressly covers hospitalization of 1 or more, amputation, and eye loss. The reporting card applies -.05 on its own terms and discloses the inconsistency rather than silently harmonizing it.
- The emergency-action and fire-prevention plan sections apply when another standard requires the plan; the small-employer oral option does not independently trigger a plan duty.
- The extinguisher full-evacuation exemption requires all conditions in 1910.157(b)(1), including no available extinguishers and a written policy. An evacuation preference alone is not enough.
- Ionizing radiation includes X-rays, but the federal section does not impose automatic badges on every dental worker or make every dental machine automatically exempt in an agreement state.
