# Employee exposure and medical record access

**Rule.** The employer must preserve covered exposure and medical records and provide employees and authorized representatives access under the rule. The employer must explain delays when timely access is not reasonably possible and provide required information about record availability.

**Exact text.**
> "Each employee exposure record shall be preserved and maintained for at least thirty (30) years, except that:" (29 CFR 1910.1020(d)(1)(ii)).

**Thresholds and deadlines.**
- Medical records: employment plus 30 years; exposure records and analyses: at least 30 years, subject to specific exceptions (29 CFR 1910.1020(d)(1)(i)-(iii)).
- If access cannot reasonably be provided within 15 working days, explain the reason and earliest availability within those 15 working days (29 CFR 1910.1020(e)(1)(i)).

**Exceptions.**
- Specific standards may set different preservation rules. General medical exceptions include separately held health-insurance claims, qualifying minor first-aid records, and records of employment shorter than 1 year given to the employee at termination. SDSs need not be retained indefinitely if identity, location, and use-time records are kept for the required period (29 CFR 1910.1020(d)(1)(i)(A)-(C), (ii)(B)). Do not use general exceptions to discard records another standard specifically requires.

**Office checklist.**
- Practice workflow: Amanda: maintain a secure record index and access-request workflow; inform covered employees initially and at least annually of record existence, custodian, and access rights. Owner dentist: approve disclosures, preserve confidentiality, and arrange transfer on closure.

**Records to keep.** Use the preservation periods above and any more specific standard. On closure transfer records to the successor; if none, notify affected current employees of access rights at least 3 months before cessation (29 CFR 1910.1020(h)(1)-(2)).
