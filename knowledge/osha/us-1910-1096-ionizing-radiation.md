# Ionizing radiation and conditional personnel monitoring

**Rule.** The employer must evaluate ionizing-radiation hazards and provide personnel monitoring when the stated exposure conditions apply, unless a compliance provision applies. The employer may rely on the section's NRC or agreement-state compliance provisions only when their licensing, registration, and compliance conditions are met.

**Exact text.**
> "Radiation includes alpha rays, beta rays, gamma rays, X-rays, neutrons, high-speed electrons, high-speed protons, and other atomic particles;" (29 CFR 1910.1096(a)(1)).

**Thresholds and deadlines.**
- Monitor restricted-area employees likely to receive more than 25% of the applicable quarterly limit; for employees under 18, more than 5%; also monitor anyone entering a high radiation area (29 CFR 1910.1096(d)(2)(i)-(iii)).
- Apply the body-part-specific quarterly dose limits in Table G-18 when determining the monitoring trigger; the higher-dose alternative has additional conditions (29 CFR 1910.1096(b)(1)-(2), Table G-18).
- Employees under 18: no more than 10% of Table G-18 limits per calendar quarter (29 CFR 1910.1096(b)(3)).

**Exceptions.**
- Paragraph (p)(1) addresses NRC-licensed atomic material; (p)(2) NRC plant/facility contractors; (p)(3)(i) agreement-state atomic material; (p)(3)(ii) other radiation sources registered or licensed with an agreement state and operated under its laws. Tennessee appears in (p)(3)(i). This does not automatically exempt every dental X-ray machine or require a badge for every dental worker.

**Office checklist.**
- Practice workflow: Amanda: collect machine registration/license and radiation-survey evidence; identify workers entering restricted/high-radiation areas. Owner dentist: obtain qualified evaluation, document the actual compliance path, and approve monitoring arrangements.

**Records to keep.** Keep exposure records for employees requiring monitoring and advise each of individual exposure at least annually; furnish a requested former-employee exposure report within 30 days. This section gives no fixed retention duration (29 CFR 1910.1096(n), (o)(1)); see the separate exposure-record card.
