# Written hazard communication program

**Rule.** The employer must develop, implement, and maintain a written hazard communication program at each workplace. The employer must include a chemical list linked to SDS product identifiers and describe labeling, SDS access, training, and communication of non-routine-task and unlabeled-pipe hazards.

**Exact text.**
> "The employer shall make the written hazard communication program available, upon request, to employees, their designated representatives, the Assistant Secretary and the Director, in accordance with the requirements of § 1910.1020(e)." (29 CFR 1910.1200(e)(4)).

**Thresholds and deadlines.**
- Maintain the program at each workplace and make it available on request (29 CFR 1910.1200(e)(1), (4)).
- Federal update effective July 19, 2024; employer substance-related updates as necessary by November 20, 2026 and mixture-related updates as necessary by May 19, 2028 (29 CFR 1910.1200(j)(1), (2)(ii), (3)(ii)).

**Exceptions.**
- For multi-employer workplaces, add methods for other employers' SDS access, precautions, and label-system information. For employees traveling between workplaces, the program may be kept at the primary workplace (29 CFR 1910.1200(e)(2), (5)).
- Federal transition permits the current text, the text revised as of July 1, 2023, or both until the applicable compliance dates (29 CFR 1910.1200(j)(4)). General coverage exclusions also appear in (b); do not assume all dental products are excluded.

**Office checklist.**
- Practice workflow: Amanda: keep chemical identifiers matched across list, labels, and SDSs; update the program when hazards or procedures change. Owner dentist: approve the program and contractor communication methods.

**Records to keep.** Keep the current written program and chemical list; no fixed retention duration stated in this paragraph (29 CFR 1910.1200(e)).
