# Emergency action plan when required

**Rule.** The employer must maintain an emergency action plan when another OSHA general-industry standard requires one. The employer must include reporting, evacuation, critical operations, employee accounting, rescue or medical duties, contacts, alarms, and evacuation-assistance training.

**Exact text.**
> "However, an employer with 10 or fewer employees may communicate the plan orally to employees." (29 CFR 1910.38(b)).

**Thresholds and deadlines.**
- 10 or fewer employees may receive an oral plan; otherwise keep the written plan at work, available for review (29 CFR 1910.38(b)).
- Review with each covered employee on development or initial assignment, responsibility changes, and plan changes (29 CFR 1910.38(f)(1)-(3)).

**Exceptions.**
- This section does not independently require every employer to have a plan: the trigger is another standard requiring one (29 CFR 1910.38(a)). Oral permission does not remove plan contents or training duties; an extinguisher evacuation exemption separately requires a written policy (29 CFR 1910.38(b)-(f); 1910.157(b)(1)).

**Office checklist.**
- Practice workflow: Amanda: document evacuation routes, reporting, employee accounting, and contacts; arrange required employee reviews. Owner dentist: identify the triggering standard, approve the plan, and designate trained evacuation assistants.

**Records to keep.** Keep the written plan in the workplace when required; no fixed archival retention stated (29 CFR 1910.38(b)).
