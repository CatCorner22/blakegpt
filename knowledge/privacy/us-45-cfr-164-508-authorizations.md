# Written authorizations

**Rule.** A covered practice must obtain a valid authorization for PHI uses or disclosures not otherwise permitted or required. The practice must keep its use within the authorization and must not use a general treatment consent as a substitute.

**Exact text.**
> "The authorization must be written in plain language." (45 CFR 164.508(c)(3)).

**Thresholds and deadlines.**
- Check each authorization for the information, sender, recipient, purpose, expiration date/event, signature and date, and representative authority when applicable (164.508(c)(1)).
- Include revocation, conditioning, and redisclosure statements; give a copy if the practice seeks the authorization (164.508(c)(2), (4)).
- Reject expired, incomplete, revoked, improperly combined/conditioned, or materially false forms (164.508(b)(2)).

**Exceptions.** 
- Psychotherapy notes, marketing, and sales have specific rules and limited exceptions under (a)(2)–(4).
- Patients may revoke in writing, except for action already taken in reliance and the insurance exception (b)(5).
- Do not condition treatment on authorization except within the narrow exceptions in (b)(4).

**Office checklist.**
- At each authorization release, Amanda checks scope, signature, expiration, and revocations; the privacy official approves unclear requests.
- Amanda gives the patient a copy of an authorization the practice solicits.

**Records to keep.** Keep signed authorizations under 164.508(b)(6) for 6 years from creation or last effective date, whichever is later (164.530(j)(2)).
