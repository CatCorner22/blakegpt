# Public health law enforcement and subpoenas

**Rule.** A covered practice may disclose PHI without patient authorization only within a specific public-interest permission and its conditions. Staff must not treat a badge or ordinary subpoena as blanket authority to release the chart.

**Exact text.**
> "the covered entity discloses only the protected health information expressly authorized by such order" (45 CFR 164.512(e)(1)(i)).

**Thresholds and deadlines.**
- Required-by-law disclosures must stay within that law's requirements (164.512(a)).
- Public-health disclosures may go to an authority legally authorized to collect the information (164.512(b)(1)(i)).
- A civil subpoena without a court order requires satisfactory assurances about patient notice or a qualified protective order, or the practice's own reasonable efforts meeting (e)(1)(vi).
- Law-enforcement requests must fit the applicable conditions of (f)(1)–(6); there is no general police-access right.
- Final rule 2026-23429 is scheduled for publication 2026-11-19; effective 60 days after publication (derived 2027-01-18), compliance 240 days after publication (derived 2027-07-17). These calendar dates are calculated, not quoted (final rule DATES, p. 1).
- Pending amendment 7 revises oversight/specialized-government provisions and adds (m); use the operative text for the particular request, not this short overview.

**Exceptions.** 
- Other laws may impose stricter limits. The vacated reproductive-health rule does not remove ordinary HIPAA limits (HHS Purl excerpt).

**Office checklist.**
- For every subpoena or police request, Amanda preserves the request and sends it to the privacy official for legal review before release.
- For every approved disclosure, Amanda verifies the requester's authority under 164.514(h), limits scope, and checks accounting requirements.

**Records to keep.** No retention rule in 164.512 itself; keep required accounting records under 164.528(d) and 164.530(j) for 6 years.
