# Tennessee work authorization and E-Verify

**Rule.** A covered private employer must verify covered new employees using E-Verify if it has 35 or more full-time equivalent employees. Below that threshold a covered private employer may use E-Verify or collect a qualifying document. A covered employer must collect a qualifying document for an individual nonemployee it pays directly for labor or services before the individual starts work.

**Exact text.**

> “remuneration paid to at least six (6) employees” (T.C.A. § 50-1-702(13) — unofficial Justia mirror)

> “thirty-five (35) or more full-time equivalent employees” (T.C.A. § 50-1-703(b) — unofficial Justia mirror)

> “prior to the employee providing labor or services” (T.C.A. § 50-1-703(a)(1)(B)(i) — unofficial Justia mirror)

**Thresholds and deadlines.**

- Private-employer coverage starts at 6 employees whose remuneration must be reported under federal law (T.C.A. § 50-1-702(13)).
- The mandatory private E-Verify threshold is 35 full-time equivalents for hires on or after January 1, 2023. The 2022 amendment replaced the prior threshold of 50 employees (2021 T.C.A. § 50-1-703(b), unofficial Justia historical mirror, and 2022 Tenn. Pub. Acts ch. 832, § 2). The state agency counts employees under the same federal employer identification number, including out-of-state employees (Tennessee Labor and Workforce Development, Explanation of 2023 TLEA Act, agency guidance).
- Obtain 1 permitted document before labor or services under the document route, including directly paid individual nonemployees (T.C.A. §§ 50-1-702(11), 50-1-703(a)(1)). An employer using the E-Verify route enrolls before hiring and keeps a case result with visible work authorization status (T.C.A. § 50-1-703(a)(1)(B)(ii)).
- The 2026 E-Verify expansion applies to the covered local governments and local education agencies and leaves the private threshold in place (2026 Tenn. Pub. Acts ch. 772, §§ 1, 3).

**Exceptions.**

- An employer without internet access or with fewer than 35 full-time equivalents may request no-cost state assistance. The statute protects an employer that has requested qualifying assistance and has yet to receive it (T.C.A. § 50-1-703(a)(3), (6)(B)).
- Federal Form I-9 duties apply separately, including below the state threshold.

**Office checklist.**

- At each hire or directly paid individual contractor engagement, check the applicable verification route before work starts. Have the owner approve the headcount method and recheck it when staffing changes.
- Restrict access to identity documents and case results. Keep them in secure office records.

**Records to keep.** Keep E-Verify results for the later of 3 years after hire or 1 year after termination. Keep statutory document copies for the later of 3 years after receipt or 1 year after the person stops providing services (T.C.A. § 50-1-703(a)(4)(A)–(B)).
