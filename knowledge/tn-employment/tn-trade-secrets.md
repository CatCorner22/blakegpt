# Tennessee trade secret protection

**Rule.** A person must not misappropriate a trade secret through improper acquisition or unauthorized use or disclosure under the statutory definition. A practice may seek an injunction for actual or threatened misappropriation and may recover qualifying damages.

**Exact text.**

> “efforts that are reasonable under the circumstances to maintain its secrecy” (T.C.A. § 47-25-1702(4)(B) — unofficial Justia mirror)

> “Actual or threatened misappropriation may be enjoined.” (T.C.A. § 47-25-1703(a) — unofficial Justia mirror)

> “within three (3) years after the misappropriation is discovered” (T.C.A. § 47-25-1707 — unofficial Justia mirror)

**Thresholds and deadlines.**

- The information must derive actual or potential independent economic value from secrecy and be subject to reasonable secrecy efforts (T.C.A. § 47-25-1702(4)).
- Bring a claim within 3 years of actual discovery or when reasonable diligence would have revealed the misappropriation. Continuing misappropriation is a single claim against each person (T.C.A. § 47-25-1707).

**Exceptions.**

- Exceptional circumstances may support a reasonable royalty in place of prohibiting future use (T.C.A. § 47-25-1703(b)).
- The damages provision allows an equitable limitation when the defendant materially and prejudicially changed position before knowledge or reason to know of misappropriation (T.C.A. § 47-25-1704(a)).

**Office checklist.**

- When granting access, document which business information the practice protects and have the owner approve role-based access. At departure, remove access, recover office copies, and preserve evidence of suspected misuse.

**Records to keep.** No retention rule in this source.
