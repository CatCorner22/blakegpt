# Federal PUMP Act breaks and private space

**Rule.** A covered employer must provide reasonable breaks each time an employee needs to express milk during the 1st year after birth and must provide a private space outside a bathroom. A covered employer must count the break as work time if the employee is not completely relieved of duty throughout the break.

**Exact text.**

> “for 1 year after the child’s birth each time such employee has need to express the milk” (29 U.S.C. § 218d(a)(1) — Cornell LII mirror)

> “other than a bathroom, that is shielded from view and free from intrusion” (29 U.S.C. § 218d(a)(2) — Cornell LII mirror)

**Thresholds and deadlines.**

- The federal duty applies for 1 year after birth. Breaks must match actual need, and compensability depends on complete relief from duty and other applicable pay law (29 U.S.C. § 218d(a)–(b)).

**Exceptions.**

- An employer with fewer than 50 employees qualifies for the exception only if compliance causes significant difficulty or expense in relation to size, resources, nature, or structure (29 U.S.C. § 218d(c)).

**Office checklist.**

- For each request, set up a private non-bathroom space and route clinical and phone coverage elsewhere. Have payroll sign off on any worked pumping time each pay cycle.

**Records to keep.** No retention rule in this source.
