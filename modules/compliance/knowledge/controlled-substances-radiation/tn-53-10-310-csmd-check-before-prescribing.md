# Check the Tennessee CSMD before prescribing opioids or benzodiazepines

**Rule.** A dentist must check the Controlled Substance Monitoring Database before prescribing an opioid or benzodiazepine at the start of a new episode of treatment, before each new prescription for that substance during the first 90 days, and at least every 6 months while it remains part of treatment. A registered delegate may run the check for the dentist.

**Exact text.**

> "shall check the controlled substance database prior to prescribing one (1) of the controlled substances identified in subdivision (e)(4) to a human patient at the beginning of a new episode of treatment, prior to the issuance of each new prescription for the controlled substance for the first ninety (90) days of a new episode of treatment, and shall check the controlled substance database for that human patient at least every six (6) months when that prescribed controlled substance remains part of the treatment." (T.C.A. § 53-10-310(e)(1), Justia unofficial mirror).

> "An authorized healthcare practitioner's delegate may check the controlled substance database on behalf of the healthcare practitioner." (T.C.A. § 53-10-310(e)(1), Justia unofficial mirror).

> "include, but are not limited to, all opioids and benzodiazepines" (T.C.A. § 53-10-310(e)(4), Justia unofficial mirror).

> "adequate for a single, three-day treatment period" (T.C.A. § 53-10-310(e)(6)(B), Justia unofficial mirror).

**Thresholds and deadlines.**

- Before the first prescription of a new episode.
- Before each new prescription during the first 90 days.
- At least every 6 months after that while the drug remains in treatment.

**Exceptions.**

- Patient in hospice care (§ 53-10-310(e)(6)(A)).
- Quantity adequate for a single 3-day treatment period with no refill (§ 53-10-310(e)(6)(B)).
- Direct administration during inpatient or residential treatment in a hospital or nursing home licensed under title 68 (§ 53-10-310(e)(6)(C)).

**Office checklist.**

- Dentist or registered delegate runs the check and notes it in the chart before the prescription is signed.
- Amanda audits 10 opioid charts per quarter for a documented check.

**Records to keep.**

No retention rule in this source; document each check in the patient record.
