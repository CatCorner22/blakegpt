# CDC 2016 Summary and 2003 dental guideline: administrative program, training and status as guidance

**Rule.** This is guidance. Tennessee law makes CDC practice binding only where 0460-01-.11 points to it (hand hygiene, PPE). The office may adopt the CDC program elements: 1 trained infection prevention coordinator, written policies reviewed at least annually, and training at hire, with new tasks, and at least annually.

**Exact text.**

> "which although archived, continues to serve as the standard of practice for clinical dentistry" (CDC Summary web page, At a Glance).

> "Assign at least one individual trained in infection prevention responsibility for coordinating the program." (CDC 2016 Summary, Key Administrative Recommendations, item 3).

> "Policies and procedures should be tailored to the dental setting and reassessed on a regular basis (e.g., annually) or according to state or federal requirements." (CDC 2016 Summary, Administrative Measures).

> "Education and training should be provided during orientation to the setting, when new tasks or procedures are introduced and at a minimum, annually." (CDC 2016 Summary, Infection Prevention Education and Training).

**Thresholds and deadlines.**

- Coordinator: at least 1 trained person.
- Policy review: e.g., annually.
- Training: orientation, new tasks, at least annually.

**Exceptions.**

- Guidance only; none stated.

**Office checklist.**

- Name the infection prevention coordinator in writing (Amanda or the lead assistant).
- Run the CDC Appendix A checklist each year; the lead dentist signs the result.
- Log annual IPC training with date, topics and attendees.

**Records to keep.** CDC: "Maintain training records according to state and federal requirements" (2016 Summary, Education and Training item 4). OSHA bloodborne training records: 3 years (see us-osha-1910-1030-h-records).
