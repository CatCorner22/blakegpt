# Fire prevention plan when required

**Rule.** The employer must maintain a fire prevention plan when another OSHA general-industry standard requires one. The employer must identify major fire hazards, handling and ignition controls, fire-protection equipment, waste controls, maintenance, and responsible employees.

**Exact text.**
> "An employer must have a fire prevention plan when an OSHA standard in this part requires one." (29 CFR 1910.39(a)).

**Thresholds and deadlines.**
- 10 or fewer employees may receive the plan orally; otherwise keep a written plan at work for review (29 CFR 1910.39(b)).
- Inform employees of exposed fire hazards at initial assignment and review self-protection provisions (29 CFR 1910.39(d)).

**Exceptions.**
- The section applies when another standard requires a plan, not universally by itself (29 CFR 1910.39(a)).

**Office checklist.**
- Practice workflow: Amanda: list hazards and fuel/ignition controls and maintain named responsible roles. Owner dentist: identify the plan trigger, approve controls, and confirm initial employee information.

**Records to keep.** Keep the written plan in the workplace when required; no fixed archival retention stated (29 CFR 1910.39(b)).
