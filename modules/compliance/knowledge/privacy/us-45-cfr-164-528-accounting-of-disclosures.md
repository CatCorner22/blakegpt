# Accounting of disclosures

**Rule.** A covered practice must provide an accounting of covered disclosures, including those by business associates, when a patient requests it. Staff must not promise a log of every use or every treatment disclosure.

**Exact text.**
> "The covered entity must provide the first accounting to an individual in any 12 month period without charge." (45 CFR 164.528(c)(2)).

**Thresholds and deadlines.**
- Look back 6 years before the request, or the shorter period requested (164.528(a)(1), (3)).
- Act within 60 days; only 1 extension up to 30 days, with written reasons and a completion date during the original period (c)(1)).
- The first accounting in 12 months is free; later requests may carry a reasonable cost-based fee after advance notice and an opportunity to withdraw/modify (c)(2)).
- Include date, recipient and known address, information description, and purpose/legal basis (b)(2)).

**Exceptions.** 
- Excluded categories include treatment/payment/operations, patient disclosures, incidental disclosures, authorizations, directory/family-care disclosures, national security, qualifying custody disclosures, limited data sets, and pre-compliance disclosures (a)(1)(i)–(ix).
- A health-oversight/law-enforcement suspension requires the conditions in (a)(2); an oral suspension lasts no longer than 30 days without a written statement.

**Office checklist.**
- For each accountable release, Amanda logs the required fields and obtains BA data when needed.
- At each request, Amanda starts the deadline tracker; the privacy official approves the accounting and any extension.

**Records to keep.** Keep accountable-disclosure data, issued accountings, and responsible office titles for 6 years under 164.528(d) and 164.530(j).
