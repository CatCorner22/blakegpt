# Blood, bone marrow, and living organ donation leave

**Rule.** A private practice may provide blood or bone marrow donation leave under its own policy or agreement. A covered employer must evaluate a qualifying serious health condition under federal family and medical leave law.

**Exact text.**

> “methods, procedures, and standards for all state agencies” (Tennessee Department of Human Resources, Attendance and Leave Manual, Introduction (public-employer guidance))

> “employed full-time with the state” (2026 Tenn. Pub. Acts ch. 779, § 1(b)(1)(A))

> “inpatient care in a hospital” (29 U.S.C. § 2611(11) — Cornell LII mirror)

**Thresholds and deadlines.**

- The state workforce manual lists a blood donation policy. The 2026 living-organ donation law grants 4 weeks for recovery to eligible employees and defines eligibility by 12 consecutive full-time months with specified state entities. Its Title 50 provision uses that same definition (2026 Tenn. Pub. Acts ch. 779, §§ 1–2).

**Exceptions.**

- Federal family or medical leave can apply if a procedure or recovery satisfies the serious-health-condition rule and both employer and employee meet federal coverage (29 U.S.C. §§ 2611, 2612(a)(1)(D)).

**Office checklist.**

- When a request arrives, document the requested leave and have the owner review office policy and any applicable leave or accommodation duties.

**Records to keep.** No retention rule in this source.

**Related.** [tn-private-sick-leave](tn-private-sick-leave.md), [us-family-medical-leave](us-family-medical-leave.md).

**Not verified.** A generally applicable Tennessee private-employer blood or bone marrow donation leave entitlement could not be confirmed. The state employee manual and 2026 living-organ law address specified state employment.
