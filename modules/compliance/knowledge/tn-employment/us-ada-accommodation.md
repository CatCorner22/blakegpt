# Federal disability accommodations

**Rule.** A covered employer must make reasonable accommodations for known limitations of an otherwise qualified applicant or employee with a disability unless the employer demonstrates undue hardship.

**Exact text.**

> “15 or more employees for each working day in each of 20 or more calendar weeks” (42 U.S.C. § 12111(5)(A) — Cornell LII mirror)

> “not making reasonable accommodations to the known physical or mental limitations” (42 U.S.C. § 12112(b)(5)(A) — Cornell LII mirror)

**Thresholds and deadlines.**

- Coverage generally starts at 15 employees on each working day in 20 or more calendar weeks in the current or preceding calendar year (42 U.S.C. § 12111(5)(A)).
- A qualified individual can perform the position's essential functions with or without reasonable accommodation (42 U.S.C. § 12111(8)).

**Exceptions.**

- The employer may decline an accommodation it can demonstrate would impose undue hardship, meaning significant difficulty or expense under the statutory factors (42 U.S.C. §§ 12111(10), 12112(b)(5)(A)).

**Office checklist.**

- At each request, review the essential duties and feasible changes with the employee. Have the owner approve the decision before changing assignments or denying the request.

**Records to keep.** Keep employee medical information on separate forms and in separate medical files, subject to the listed disclosure exceptions (42 U.S.C. § 12112(d)(3)(B), (4)(C)). No retention rule in this source.
