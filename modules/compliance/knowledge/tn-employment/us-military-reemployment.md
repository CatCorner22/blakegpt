# Federal uniformed-service reemployment

**Rule.** An employer must not deny employment benefits based on covered uniformed service and must reemploy eligible returning service members. An employee must give advance notice of service and report back or apply for reemployment within the applicable period, subject to the statutory exceptions.

**Exact text.**

> “shall be entitled to the reemployment rights and benefits” (38 U.S.C. § 4312(a) — Cornell LII mirror)

> “State active duty for a period of 14 days or more” (38 U.S.C. § 4303(13) — Cornell LII mirror)

> “does not exceed five years” (38 U.S.C. § 4312(a)(2) — Cornell LII mirror)

**Thresholds and deadlines.**

- The general cumulative service limit with an employer is 5 years, subject to excluded service under 38 U.S.C. § 4312(c).
- Covered state active duty includes 14 days or more and duty in response to a presidentially declared national emergency or qualifying major disaster (38 U.S.C. § 4303(13)).
- For service under 31 days, the first full scheduled work period follows safe travel home and 8 hours. For 31–180 days, apply within 14 days. For longer than 180 days, apply within 90 days. Special timing applies to fitness examinations and service-related illness or injury (38 U.S.C. § 4312(e)).

**Exceptions.**

- Military necessity and impossibility or unreasonableness can excuse advance notice (38 U.S.C. § 4312(b)).
- A limited defense can apply if reemployment is impossible or unreasonable due to changed circumstances, would impose undue hardship for specified placements, or the prior job was brief and nonrecurrent with no reasonable expectation of continuation (38 U.S.C. § 4312(d)).

**Office checklist.**

- At each military absence, log the notice, classify the orders, and submit any disputed eligibility or return placement to the owner before taking action.
